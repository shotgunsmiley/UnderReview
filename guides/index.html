Before you read a review

Who holds the keys?

"Non-custodial" protocols keep funds in your personal Web3 wallet until a smart contract executes the wager. A custodial platform uses account balances: you deposit into a platform-controlled wallet before playing.

Licensing and oversight

See the methodology answer: How do I check a casino's licence on a regulator's register?

Verification (KYC)

Even when platforms advertise no-KYC or minimal-KYC onboarding for routine play, terms of service may allow identity and source-of-funds checks upon large withdrawals or automated AML risk flags. See KYC / AML in the glossary.

Provably fair explained

In traditional online casinos, you have to trust that the company's server didn't rig the spin. Provably fair uses cryptographic hashing (like a sealed envelope) so neither the casino nor the player can change the outcome after a bet is placed.

  1. Server seed hash locked. Before you bet, the casino picks a secret random number (server seed) and shows you its scrambled fingerprint (SHA-256 hash). Like putting the answer inside a locked glass box before the game starts.
  2. Your player seed added. Your browser adds your own random text (client seed) plus your bet counter (nonce). Because the casino couldn't predict your seed when it locked step 1, it cannot pre-rig the result.
  3. Outcome revealed and verified. After the round, the casino reveals the unscrambled server seed. Any independent tool can re-run the SHA-256 math to confirm it matches step 1's fingerprint.

See Provably fair in the glossary, and Common questions for what it does and does not prove.

Smart-contract casinos

In a non-custodial smart contract casino, you never deposit money into a company's bank account. Instead, your wallet interacts directly with code.

Direct bet signature

Funds stay in your wallet until the exact second a wager executes.

Three key risks of on-chain casinos

  1. Smart contract exploits. If the underlying code has an unaudited bug, hackers can drain the shared liquidity pool—and blockchain transactions cannot be reversed. See Liquidity Pool.
  2. Randomness and oracle manipulation. Poorly designed contracts that rely on block timestamps instead of verifiable randomness (VRF) can be gamed by validators or bots.
  3. Liquidity provider drawdowns. Users who stake funds to "be the house" face real financial loss during whale winning streaks or token price drops.

Wallet security

In Web3 gaming, you are your own bank.

  • Use a dedicated "burner" gaming wallet. Never connect your main long-term hardware vault directly to any gaming dApp. Keep only your session bankroll in a separate hot wallet.
  • Cap and revoke token spending approvals. Avoid signing "Unlimited USDC Approval" prompts. Periodically revoke stale permissions.
  • Bookmark official domains (stop search-ad phishing). Scammers buy lookalike sponsored ads on search engines that host wallet-drainer clones. Always verify the exact URL.
  • Verify network and withdrawal address before sending. Confirm both sender and receiver are on the exact same chain (e.g., Arbitrum USDC vs. Ethereum Mainnet) and send a small test amount first.
  • Enable hardware passkeys or app-based 2FA. For custodial accounts, use authenticator apps or FIDO2 passkeys instead of SMS 2FA to prevent SIM-swap account takeovers.

Custodial or self-custody

  • Custodial account (email/login): the platform holds the keys. The operator can freeze withdrawals, delay KYC reviews, or become insolvent.
  • Self-custody wallet: you hold the keys. If you lose your recovery phrase or sign a malicious contract, there is no helpdesk undo button.

Scam patterns

Fraudulent operators recycle the same playbooks under new brand names. Learn four recurring patterns to watch for before depositing a single dollar.

1. Fake footer licence seals

How it works: scam sites paste static PNG images of Curaçao, MGA, or UKGC badges in their footer without an active validator link.

How to check: see How do I check a casino's licence on a regulator's register?

2. Withdrawal-trap KYC and "tax" fees

How it works: a site lets you deposit and "win" huge balances effortlessly, then demands an upfront "verification deposit" or "unlock tax" before releasing funds.

How to spot it: be wary of any request to deposit extra money before a withdrawal.

3. "Guaranteed yield / AI arbitrage" betting pools

How it works: promoters claim an "AI sports-betting bot" generates guaranteed daily returns if you stake tokens into their house pool.

How to spot it: no betting algorithm eliminates variance.

4. Fake airdrop and "free spin" wallet drainers

How it works: social media replies offer "free bonus chips" that prompt your wallet to sign an opaque Permit2 or setApprovalForAll transaction.

How to spot it: be wary of any prompt to sign a token-transfer permission to claim a bonus.
